My name is Noah.

I want one place where I can:
- Track income, expenses and my recorded balance.
- Set monthly spending limits for different categories.
- Log what I spend each day.
- Record daily steps and set my own step goal.
- Track calories by searching for food or scanning a product.
- Reuse favourite foods and meals without looking up their calories again.
- Review previous days, weeks and months.

Example:
I set a food budget of 2,300 NOK per month. Whenever I buy food, I enter the amount. The app immediately shows how much I have spent and how much remains.

Separately, when I eat or drink something, I search for it or scan its barcode, choose the amount consumed, and the app calculates and logs the calories.

Buying food and consuming food are different events. Never automatically turn one into the other.

Working app name: “Noah Tracker”.
You may propose a better name with the design options, but do not turn naming into a separate project.

4. VISUAL AND MOTION QUALITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Make this look like a thoughtfully designed personal product with its own identity.

Avoid:
- A generic AI-generated dashboard.
- Endless identical cards in a standard grid.
- Random purple gradients and excessive glass effects.
- Stock photographs of people.
- Decorative graphs with no useful purpose.
- Tiny text, cramped spacing and excessive visual noise.
- A landing page instead of a functioning application.

Prioritise:
Strong typography, deliberate spacing, clear hierarchy, readable numbers, restrained colour, coherent icons and useful interactions.

Design around the questions:
“What have I spent?”
“What do I have left?”
“How am I doing today?”
“How do I quickly add something?”

Use colour meaningfully, but also communicate status through text and labels.

Make mobile a first-class experience. Use navigation and input flows that suit a phone instead of squeezing the desktop layout onto a smaller screen.

Design loading, empty, error, success and over-budget states as carefully as the populated overview.

Respect keyboard navigation, visible focus, screen-reader labels, sufficient contrast and reduced-motion preferences.

MOTION REQUIREMENTS — APPLY ANIMATE

The tracker should feel calm, responsive and deliberately designed during repeated daily use. More animation is not automatically better. Make an intentional choice between a transition and an immediate state change.

Prioritise these interactions where motion genuinely helps:
- Opening and closing expense forms, food details and mobile sheets.
- Switching dates, months or views without losing context.
- Clear button, favourite and save/error feedback.
- Updating progress indicators after a real change in the underlying records.

Keep movement restrained. Avoid bouncy motion, generic fade-up on every element, excessive stagger, long page introductions, looping decoration, scroll hijacking and large hover movements. Essential actions and information must never wait for an entrance animation.

Use a small, consistent motion system rather than unrelated timings on each component. Choose suitable durations and easing from the actual Animate guidance and the approved direction; do not apply one arbitrary duration to everything.

Reuse the project's animation approach when suitable. Use simple CSS transitions for simple interactions and introduce a motion library only when the approved interaction genuinely needs it. Do not add multiple animation libraries for the same purpose. Prefer transform/opacity where appropriate and avoid unnecessary layout shifts.

Animations must remain interruptible. Rapidly opening and closing a sheet, changing tabs or editing a value must not leave stale overlays, lost focus, blocked controls or duplicate submissions.

Keep calculations, persistence and displayed truth independent of animation. Do not delay a save to finish an effect, report success before storage succeeds, or count financial/calorie totals up from fabricated values. Authoritative numbers should be readable immediately; any progress animation must end at the correct underlying value.

Respect prefers-reduced-motion with reduced or instant transitions. Preserve meaningful state feedback when motion is removed. Support keyboard and touch, return focus correctly when dialogs close, and never rely on hover or animation alone to communicate status.

Before handover, use Impeccable to critique the built screens and Animate to review the live interactions. Fix the highest-impact issues without abandoning the approved design. Do not require an additional review-animations skill to perform this review. Report anything that could not be tested rather than calling it polished or verified.
